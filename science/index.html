Evidence first, claims second.

How StrideAI measures, what is validated, what is still being tested, and the words we use for each.

Questions we test

Validation, by question.

Does the insole record what a lab system records?

StrideAI's validation study compares insole values with reference measures (for example a stopwatch, walkway or lab system) using agreement statistics: bias, 95 percent limits, mean absolute error and intraclass correlation. Results will be published here when available.

Are both feet recorded at the same moment?

Left and right shoes are aligned to one clock, and each session reports a clock alignment check. Two-footed landings are used as a field test: both feet must agree within 10 ms.

How fast is the data sampled?

Target: 100 samples per second per channel for timing measures. Measures that need faster sampling, such as loading rate, are not reported.

Which measures are shown, and how sure are they?

Every result carries a label: Measured, Derived or Experimental. Experimental results stay labeled until validated on real people.

What happens to the data?

The platform is designed so data is encrypted at rest, studies use participant codes, the wearer decides what any organization can see, and wearers can download or delete their data.

Status today

Where the evidence stands.

StrideAI hardware records pressure, motion, temperature and humidity on both feet. Analysis methods have been checked on public research datasets, and a validation study with people is being prepared to measure accuracy against reference instruments. Until it reports, results are described as wellness information and Experimental measures stay labeled as such.

Artificial intelligence is on the roadmap: StrideAI is acquiring high-quality, consented data first, and models follow the data.

Glossary

Terms used on this site.

Plantar pressure
Force under the sole of the foot, shown here as a share of load by zone rather than in absolute units.
Step rate (cadence)
Steps per minute.
Ground contact time
How long a foot stays on the ground in each step.
Landing impact
Peak acceleration at foot strike, in g.
Foot swing
Angular speed of the foot while it is in the air.
Bilateral capture
Recording both feet together on one clock.
PPG
Photoplethysmography: optical sensing of pulse.
EMG
Electromyography: electrical activity of muscles.
IMU
Inertial measurement unit: accelerometer, gyroscope and magnetometer.
