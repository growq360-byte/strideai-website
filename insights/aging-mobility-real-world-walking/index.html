Aging and mobility: why everyday walking data matters

StrideAI market brief. Sources checked November 20, 2026.

CDC calls falls the leading cause of injury for adults 65 and older. More than 14 million older adults, about 1 in 4, report falling each year [1]. In 2023, about 41,400 adults 65 and older died from unintentional falls, a crude rate of 69.9 per 100,000, and fall death rates rose in every age group between 2003 and 2023 [2].

Walking speed is one of the most studied signals in aging research. A pooled analysis of 9 cohorts and 34,485 adults aged 65 and older found that faster gait speed went with longer survival: each 0.1 m/s faster carried a hazard ratio of 0.88 [3].

A single walk in the clinic is not the same as daily life. A 2026 meta-analysis of 11 studies found that supervised lab gait speed averaged 0.22 m/s faster than real-world gait speed measured with wearables, and called for more standardized real-world methods [4]. Continuous in-shoe measurement during everyday walking is one way to collect that kind of data. StrideAI collects mobility data for wellness and research; it is not a fall-risk or diagnostic tool.

Sources

  1. CDC, Older Adult Falls Data (page updated Sep 4, 2026; the 14 million figure draws on 2020 to 2021 survey data). https://www.cdc.gov/falls/data-research/index.html
  2. CDC NCHS Data Brief No. 532, Unintentional Fall Deaths in Adults Age 65 and Older, 2023 (Jun 18, 2025). https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db532.htm
  3. Studenski S et al. Gait speed and survival in older adults. JAMA, 2011. PMID 21205966. https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2010.1923
  4. Nguyen TML et al. Laboratory vs daily-life gait speed in healthy older adults: systematic review and meta-analysis. Gait & Posture, 2026. PMID 42296946. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2026.110254
