Feel is not data.

White paper No. 1, November 2026. Measured fit for every athlete, from the field and the court to the halfpipe, the skatepark and the surf.

Executive summary

Sport is changing faster than the footwear behind it. Women are driving growth in performance footwear, and skateboarding, surfing and BMX freestyle are on the program for the LA28 Games.4,5 Yet the most important question in footwear, whether it fits the way this athlete actually moves, is still answered by feel.

StrideAI builds smart footwear intelligence that turns that feel into data. Its smart insole is designed to fit the shoes and boots athletes already wear and to record pressure, motion, temperature and humidity on both feet at once, in the field, not just in the lab. This paper sets out the evidence, sport by sport, and invites brands, clubs, athletes, fitters and researchers to build the dataset with us.

Key takeaways

  • Feet differ in shape, not just size. Research finds women's feet differ from men's at the arch, outer edge, big toe and forefoot, and many people wear shoes that do not fit their width or length.6,3
  • Action sports put the foot at the center. Edge control, pop, pedal feel and landings all run through the foot, inside boots and shoes still fitted mostly by feel.
  • The gap is data. Brands, teams and fitters have deep expertise; what they lack is synchronized measurement from inside the shoe or boot, on both feet, in the field.
  • StrideAI is built to measure. One insole platform for sneakers, cleats, snowboard and ski boots, skate shoes and BMX shoes, with surf as a research direction.
  • Partners shape it. StrideAI is building this dataset with partners, under clear athlete data protections.

Feet differ in shape, not just size

Even after correcting for foot length, women's and men's feet differ in several regions, most notably the arch, the outer edge, the big toe and the forefoot, and the authors conclude these differences should be considered in the design of women's sport shoes.6 Large 3D scanning studies find that, at the same foot length, men's feet are wider, fuller and taller than women's.8,9 In a small study of 20 runners, loading under the outer foot changed differently for women and men after a 5 km run.10

The missing measurement

Rigid footwear sharpens the problem. In alpine ski racing, boot selection still relies largely on the experience of a few experts, and researchers have begun turning that expertise into data-driven models.11 Pressure insoles combined with motion sensors and GPS have been used to measure forces in alpine skiing on the mountain.7 A 2026 systematic review found that wireless pressure insoles showed acceptable agreement with laboratory force plates and fair to excellent repeatability, while noting small samples and few gold-standard comparisons.12 We are not aware of a published, sex-stratified, in-shoe field dataset across the sports in this paper. That is the opportunity.

Built for women's feet from the first capture

Women athletes are intended to be founding voices of this dataset, not a subgroup added later. StrideAI records sex, sport and footwear for every session from the first capture, so the data that shapes the next generation of women's footwear starts with women riders, skaters, surfers and players.
