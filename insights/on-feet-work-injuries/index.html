Workplace: jobs done on your feet

StrideAI market brief. Sources checked November 20, 2026.

U.S. private industry employers reported 2.5 million nonfatal workplace injuries and illnesses in 2024 [1].

Two categories stand out for people who work on their feet. Overexertion, repetitive motion and bodily conditions accounted for 946,290 cases involving days away, restricted work or job transfer across 2023 to 2024, about 32% of such cases. Falls, slips and trips accounted for 721,720 such cases, including 479,480 with days away from work [1].

A NIOSH review links prolonged standing at work with low back and leg pain, cardiovascular problems and fatigue, and lists shoes and shoe inserts among interventions to consider [2]. Footwear sensing can give employers and researchers objective data on standing time, steps and load.

Sources

  1. BLS, Employer-Reported Workplace Injuries and Illnesses, 2023-2024 (Jan 22, 2026). https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/osh_01222026.htm
  2. Waters TR, Dick RB (NIOSH). Health risks of prolonged standing at work and intervention effectiveness. Rehabil Nurs, 2015. PMID 25041875. https://doi.org/10.1002/rnj.166
