Sport and athlete load: measuring where load meets the ground

StrideAI market brief. Sources checked November 20, 2026.

Running injuries are common: a meta-analysis found 17.8 running-related injuries per 1,000 hours for novice runners and 7.7 for recreational runners [1]. The International Olympic Committee consensus statement on load gives guidance on prescribing and monitoring training load, competition load and athlete well-being [2].

Teams already rely on wearables, but the data does not always lead to decisions. In a survey of high-level football clubs, all responding clubs used GPS and heart-rate monitors in every training session, yet practitioners rated monitoring less effective than they expected; the top barriers were limited staff and coach buy-in [3]. A 2024 survey of 72 team-sport practitioners found 87.5% use wearable data to plan training but only 50% use it for competition decisions [4].

Most team systems are worn on the torso, while the load of every stride passes through the foot. A footwear layer that measures pressure and motion at the ground can add context coaches can read. StrideAI's role today is data acquisition, with models on the roadmap.

  3. Akenhead R, Nassis GP. Training load and player monitoring in high-level football. Int J Sports Physiol Perform, 2016. PMID 26456711. https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2015-0331
