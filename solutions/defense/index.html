Context

The need, with sources.

Read the full brief and its sources

Initial entry training is one of the most demanding running and marching settings anywhere. A U.S. Army review of calendar year 2016 counted 65,026 injuries among 106,367 trainees; mechanical energy to the lower limbs caused 75% of all injuries, and 65% of those came from cumulative microtrauma, meaning overuse [1].

In a prospective cohort of 2,988 Army Basic Combat Training trainees (2017 to 2023), 1,487 had at least one coded musculoskeletal injury, about half the cohort [2]. In British Army basic training (2015 to 2016), women in officer training had 114 stress fractures per 1,000 versus 19 per 1,000 for men [3].

StrideAI records training load as a training and research input. It does not assess injury risk or diagnose injuries.

Data and deployment

Designed for a chain of command.

  • Designed to encrypt data at rest; studies use participant codes.
  • Each soldier decides what is shared; leaders see groups of five or more only.
  • Individual results go only to performance staff, with separate consent.
  • Local capture without a phone will use the StrideAI Capture Station, coming next; offline field operation is on the roadmap.
